STUDENT PERFORMANCE INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM

An Explainable Academic Analytics & Early Warning Platform

A holistic, rule-based decision-support system featuring multi-dimensional risk scoring, department benchmarking, student self-service simulations, and dedicated educator cockpits.

Author & Presenter Satyajeet Singh
Department Department of Data Science
Technology Stack Python, Flask, SQLite3, Pandas
Project Type Web-Based Intelligence System

1. Executive Summary & Problem Definition

Educational institutions generate vast amounts of operational data across classrooms, labs, and internal tests. However, this data often sits isolated in disparate files, leaving mentors and instructors unable to identify at-risk students before terminal examinations. Traditional machine learning attempts often act as "black-box" classifiers, failing to explain the root pedagogical cause to either the instructor or the student.

The Student Performance Intelligence System addresses this critical bottleneck by replacing obscure predictive models with a completely transparent, explainable rule-based analytical engine. The system unifies continuous assessments, attendance telemetry, lab participation, and study habits into standardized, actionable scores.

2. System Architecture & Tech Stack

The system follows a clean Model-View-Controller (MVC) architectural separation engineered in Python and lightweight web standards:

  • Application Layer: Flask 3.1 WSGI framework handling session authentication, role authorization, and REST endpoints.
  • Intelligence & Analytics Engine: Pandas and NumPy executing vectorized score formulas, percentiles, and risk matrices.
  • Persistence Layer: Embedded SQLite3 relational database (student_intelligence.db) with full referential integrity.
  • Client Presentation Layer: Responsive, zero-dependency HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript ensuring compatibility across mobile, tablet, and desktop viewports.

3. Analytical Formulations & Weights

Every student is evaluated using a weighted multi-criteria formulation where summative outcomes and engagement behaviors are balanced:

Study Score = min((Study_Hours / 6) * 100, 100)
Performance Score = (Final_Marks * 0.35) + (Attendance * 0.15) + (Study_Score * 0.10) +
                    (Assignment_Score * 0.10) + (Quiz_Average * 0.10) +
                    (Practical_Score * 0.10) + (Internal_Marks * 0.10)

5. Key Application Modules & Features

5.1 Teacher Cockpit Dashboard

  • Cohort Metric Tiles: Immediate visibility of total class enrollment, average attendance, and counts of students flagged for urgent support.
  • Local AI Assistant: Provides instant responses to natural language queries regarding class status, low attendance lists, and department breakdowns without requiring expensive third-party APIs.
  • Student Profile Deep-Dive: Generates individual diagnostic cards highlighting subject-specific strengths (≥80%) and improvement areas (<65%).

5.2 Student Self-Service Portal & What-If Simulator

  • Personalized Scorecard: Shows the student their score, department benchmark gap, and exact feedback.
  • Interactive What-If Simulator: Lets students adjust their attendance and study hour sliders to see simulated improvements in their score before final exams.
  • Study Material Bank: Instant access to course notes, revision sheets, and previous question papers.

6. Viva Voce & Oral Defense Preparation

Q1: Why was a rule-based algorithm chosen over Machine Learning models?

Answer: Educational institutions require explainability and transparency. A machine learning model like a neural network or random forest functions as a black box—it tells you a student might fail, but cannot easily audit why. Our rule-based system provides a clear, mathematical justification that both teachers and students can trust and act upon.

Q2: What is the purpose of the Department Benchmark & Performance Gap?

Answer: Grading standards often vary across departments. Comparing raw scores would produce misleading results. By subtracting the departmental mean from the student's individual score, the Performance Gap isolates whether a student is performing above or below their immediate peer group.

Q3: How is user data secured within the system?

Answer: All passwords are encrypted with one-way SHA-256 cryptographic hashes. Access is governed by strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): students can strictly only view their own records, while teachers possess class-wide analytical privileges.

Q4: How does the What-If Simulator benefit student psychology?

Answer: Traditional report cards are retrospective (they show past mistakes). The What-If Simulator is prospective: it empowers students to see how actionable habit changes today (such as increasing study time by 2 hours) will directly boost their future performance score.

Q5: How can this system be deployed for public access?

Answer: The project is fully cloud-ready with Procfile, render.yaml, and wsgi.py. By connecting the GitHub repository to cloud platforms such as Render or PythonAnywhere, it deploys automatically with a free live public URL.
